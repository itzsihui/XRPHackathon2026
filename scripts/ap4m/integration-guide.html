DEVELOPER GUIDE

AP4M developer guide

For the Mastercard XRPL Hackathon only. This SDK and guide are not production libraries and must not be used outside of the hackathon context.

AP4M is a payment primitive you embed in whatever project your team is building — an AI shopping agent, a data marketplace, a content paywall, IoT billing, anything that needs autonomous machine-to-machine payments. This guide shows you how to wire it in: your agent opens a funded payment channel, signs vouchers to pay merchants, and the service settles RLUSD on-chain. All of this runs on XRPL EVM Testnet · chain ID 1449000.

Demo 1 — Direct payment (start here)

Single file, everything pre-filled: team name, API URL, channel ID, contract address. Add your AGENT_PRIVATE_KEY and run node ap4m-agent.js. Walks through sign → redeem on-chain → settle in one go. Get it from the Quick Start section on your My Setup tab.

Demo 2 — x402 HTTP payment (full E2E)

Run a Merchant and an Agent locally — the Agent pays over HTTP 402 automatically. See x402.md for the full protocol.

How it works

AP4M gives your project a programmable payment layer. Whatever you are building — an AI agent that buys data, a service that charges per API call, a merchant that sells resources over HTTP — the flow is the same: your agent holds a funded budget, opens payment channels to merchants, and signs cryptographic vouchers to pay. Your private key never leaves your machine. The service handles settlement, transfers RLUSD to the merchant payout address, and publishes a verifiable on-chain receipt.

First, add your Agent wallet on the participant portal's My setup tab and fund your budget (from your funding wallet or the hackathon admin). Your agent then opens a payment channel with requestChannel(): a funded lane with a spending limit you choose within your budget. Pay by signing vouchers directly or over x402 HTTP — the merchant redeems them on-chain and the service settles automatically.

The direct payment flow takes these steps. Settlement is automatic — step 4 just waits for it and returns your receipt. Step 5 closes a channel early; channels close automatically at the event end.

Your agent only does steps 1–3. Settlement and payout happen automatically — step 4 just triggers them early and returns the receipt. Step 5 is optional; channels auto-close at the event end. RLUSD goes directly to the merchant payout address set at onboarding. Your private key stays on your machine — the SDK signs locally and the AP4M service never sees it.

Judging criteria

AP4M is a payment primitive — your project wraps it in a use case. Here are some examples of what teams build:

Autonomy
Does the agent decide and pay without human approval?

The agent must read the 402 response, choose to pay, sign and submit the voucher — all without a human in the loop.

Use case clarity
Is there a real reason a machine needs to pay?

AI agent buying data, paying per API call, unlocking content over x402, billing IoT sensors — the payment must be load-bearing, not cosmetic.

On-chain proof
Can you show a VoucherRedeemed event on the explorer?

The on-chain transaction hash is your receipt. Judges verify it lives on XRPL EVM Testnet at the contract address.

RLUSD settlement
Does RLUSD actually reach the merchant payout address?

Settlement must complete — not just a voucher submitted. Judges check the merchant wallet balance changed.

Demo quality
Can you run the full flow live in 3 minutes?

Agent starts, encounters a 402, pays, resource is unlocked, settlement recorded. No pre-recorded output — live on testnet.

Creativity
Is the use case novel or unexpected?

Bonus points for pushing beyond the starter kit example — new merchant types, multi-step agent reasoning, real-world data sources.

What a strong demo proves
  • Autonomous decision — the agent chose to pay without being told to, based on a 402 response it received.
  • On-chain voucher — a VoucherRedeemed event visible on the XRPL EVM Testnet explorer at the PaymentLedger contract address (shown on the Network config page).
  • Settlement complete — RLUSD moved from vault to merchant payout address; your portal shows the receipt.
  • Live flow — judges can watch the agent run, see the 402, see the payment go through, see the resource returned.
